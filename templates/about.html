About the Hospital Readmission Risk Predictor

Academic machine learning research prototype for explainable clinical risk estimation.

Non-Clinical Research Disclaimer

This application is an academic and research demonstration built using historical, de-identified electronic health records (EHR) collected between 1999 and 2008. The predictive models and SHAP explanations are not clinically validated medical devices. They must not be used for diagnosis, clinical decision-making, treatment prioritization, discharge timing, or real-time patient triage.

Dataset Lineage & Target Definition
  • Source: UCI Machine Learning Repository — Diabetes 130-US Hospitals (1999–2008).
  • Cohort Volume: 101,766 inpatient encounters across 130 clinical facilities.
  • Patient Cardinality: 71,518 unique patients (mean 1.42 encounters/patient; up to 40 repeat visits).
  • Target Definition: Binary 30-day readmission indicator (`readmitted == '<30'`). Readmissions after 30 days (`>30`) and non-readmissions (`NO`) are designated as negative.
  • Prediction Point: Formulated strictly at the time of hospital discharge.
Patient-Aware Splitting Safeguard: Models were evaluated with strict group splitting on `patient_nbr` (70% train / 15% val / 15% test) to prevent cross-partition leakage of patient histories.

Ethical Considerations & Clinical Limitations
1. Historical Temporal Mismatch

The dataset reflects clinical practices, diagnostic coding (ICD-9), and pharmacotherapy from 1999–2008. Contemporary diabetes medications (such as SGLT2 inhibitors and modern GLP-1 receptor agonists) are not represented.

2. Social Determinants of Health (SDOH)

Key drivers of readmission—such as food insecurity, housing instability, transportation barriers, and health literacy—are absent from administrative inpatient billing claims, capping maximum achievable discrimination.

3. Correlation vs. Causation

SHAP feature attributions reflect mathematical associations within the trained model, not biological mechanisms. Modifying a feature in real life does not guarantee an equivalent shift in readmission probability.
